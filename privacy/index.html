SIX AND A HALF STUDIOS

Privacy Policy

Effective date: October 22, 2026 · Applies to every game and app published by Six and a Half Studios LLC

The short version. Our games have no accounts, no sign-ins and no friends lists. Your progress lives on your device. Some of our games are free and show ads; in those, Google's advertising software collects the usual advertising data (an advertising ID, IP address, device details) and we collect anonymous gameplay statistics tied to a random install ID, never to you. You can turn personalized ads off, withdraw consent at any time, and ask us to delete the little we hold. The table at the end says exactly which of this applies to each game.

1. Who we are

Six and a Half Studios LLC ("we", "us") is a New Mexico limited liability company, 1209 Mountain Road Pl NE Ste N, Albuquerque, NM 87110, United States. We publish mobile games and apps ("our apps") on Google Play and other stores, and we run this website. For anything about privacy, write to contact@sixandahalf.studio. We are the data controller for the processing described here.

2. What this policy covers

This policy covers all of our apps and this website. Our apps fall into two groups:

  • Offline apps collect no personal data at all. Everything stays on your device.
  • Free-to-play apps are supported by advertising and optional purchases. They use advertising and analytics software as described in sections 4 to 6.

Section 13 lists each app and which group it belongs to. Where an app ever differs from this policy, its listing says so and links to the specific terms.

3. Data that stays on your device

All of our apps store game progress, settings, unlocked content, streaks, daily-allowance counters and purchase entitlements locally on your device. We hold no copy. Uninstalling the app deletes this data. Our apps request no sensitive permissions: no contacts, location, camera, microphone, photos or files.

Free-to-play apps also create a random install ID when first opened. It is a random string, generated on your device, not derived from your device or account, and it is the only identifier our own systems ever see. Reinstalling the app creates a new one.

4. Advertising (free-to-play apps)

Free-to-play apps show ads through Google AdMob, including rewarded ads you can choose to watch for in-game benefits. To request, show and measure ads, and to detect fraud, the AdMob software collects and sends to Google and its advertising partners:

  • your device's advertising ID (Google Advertising ID);
  • IP address, from which an approximate (city-level) location may be inferred;
  • device and app details: model, operating system version, language, screen size, app version, network type;
  • interactions with ads: impressions, clicks, views and rewards; and
  • your consent choices.

Google and its partners use this data to serve ads, measure them, prevent fraud and, if you have consented or not opted out, to personalize ads based on your activity across apps. Google describes its practices at How Google uses information from sites or apps that use our services, and lists its advertising partners at Ad technology providers. We do not receive the advertising data itself; we see aggregated reports and, in our own analytics, whether an ad was shown or rewarded.

Your advertising choices

  • In the app: Settings → Manage consent (or Privacy options) lets you refuse or withdraw consent to personalized ads at any time. With consent refused you still see ads, but non-personalized ones.
  • On Android: Settings → Google → Ads lets you delete or reset your advertising ID. If you delete it, the app and its ad partners receive a string of zeros instead.
  • In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland, no ad is loaded until you have made a choice in the consent dialog, which appears after your first completed puzzle, never on first launch.
  • In US states with privacy laws, the in-app privacy options act as your "Do Not Sell or Share My Personal Information" and targeted-advertising opt-out (see section 10).

5. Analytics (free-to-play apps)

To understand whether our games are fun, fair and working, free-to-play apps send us gameplay events: app opened, puzzle started or solved, time taken, hints used, stars earned, allowance used, offers seen, ads requested or watched, purchases made, settings changed, consent state. Each event carries the random install ID, the app version, the device model and OS version, your country (derived from your IP address when the event is received; the IP address itself is discarded), and the experiment groups your install was randomly assigned to.

These events contain no name, email, account, precise location, advertising ID or contacts. We use them for aggregate statistics, to balance difficulty and the daily allowance, and to compare variants of the game (A/B tests). We do not use them to build a profile of you and we do not sell them. Analytics runs on PostHog software operated for us by PostHog Inc. in the United States. Nothing is sent before you have made your consent choice, and withdrawing consent in Settings stops it.

6. Remote configuration

At launch, free-to-play apps download a small configuration file from our servers (game tuning, allowance values, experiment definitions). Like any web request, this exposes your IP address and the app version to our server, whose access logs are kept for up to 30 days for security and troubleshooting and are not combined with analytics.

7. Purchases

Some apps offer in-app purchases (puzzle packs, hint bundles, time-limited or permanent passes). Purchases are processed entirely by Google Play under Google's Privacy Policy. We never see your payment details. We receive a purchase token, the product bought and its status, which we use to grant and restore what you bought. Refunds are handled through Google Play.

8. Other data

  • Crash and performance data. Our apps include no crash-reporting software. Google Play provides us with aggregated crash and performance statistics for Android devices that have opted in to usage and diagnostics sharing with Google.
  • Sharing. Features such as sharing a result card create the image on your device and hand it to the app you choose through your operating system's share sheet. Nothing is sent to us.
  • Support. If you email us, we keep the correspondence for up to two years so we can help you and recognize repeat issues.
  • This website. A static site with no accounts, no analytics and no advertising. It is hosted on GitHub Pages; GitHub logs visitors' IP addresses for security purposes under the GitHub Privacy Statement. We do not receive those logs.

9. Children

Our apps are not directed at children under 13 (or the age below which parental consent is required where you live), and we do not knowingly collect personal data from them. Our free-to-play apps are published outside Google Play's Families program. If a device or store account signals that the user is a child, our apps request only non-personalized, child-appropriate ads. If you believe a child has provided us personal data, write to us and we will delete it.

10. Your rights and choices

Everyone

  • Manage consent for personalized ads and analytics any time in Settings.
  • Delete local data by uninstalling the app.
  • Delete the data we hold. Settings → Delete my data shows your install ID and lets you email us; we delete every analytics event tied to that ID within 30 days. Because we hold nothing that identifies you, the install ID is the only way to find your data, and we will ask for it rather than for your identity.

European Economic Area, United Kingdom and Switzerland

Our legal bases are: your consent for personalized advertising and for analytics; our legitimate interests in showing non-personalized ads, preventing fraud and keeping our apps secure and working; and performance of a contract for purchases. You have the rights of access, rectification, erasure, restriction, portability and objection, and the right to withdraw consent without affecting earlier processing. Write to us to exercise them. You may also complain to your local supervisory authority. Our analytics and configuration servers are in the United States. Google's processing is covered by its own transfer safeguards; for ours we rely on your explicit consent and, with any service provider we use, the standard contractual clauses.

California and other US states

In the past 12 months our free-to-play apps have collected these categories of personal information: identifiers (advertising ID, random install ID, IP address), internet or app activity (gameplay and ad interaction events), approximate geolocation (from IP address), commercial information (purchases) and, on the part of advertising partners, inferences for ad personalization. We collect them from your device, for the purposes in sections 4 to 7. We do not sell personal information for money and we collect no sensitive personal information. Personalized advertising through AdMob can count as "sharing" or "sale" for cross-context behavioral advertising under state law; to opt out, use Manage consent / Privacy options in the app, which turns off personalized advertising, or delete your advertising ID in Android settings. You also have the rights to know, delete and correct your personal information, and not to be discriminated against for exercising them. Submit requests to contact@sixandahalf.studio, including your install ID from Settings; an authorized agent may submit on your behalf with your written permission. We do not knowingly sell or share personal information of consumers under 16.

11. How long we keep data

  • Local game data: on your device until you uninstall.
  • Analytics events: up to 24 months, then deleted or reduced to aggregates that contain no install ID.
  • Server logs: up to 30 days.
  • Purchase records: as long as needed to honor your purchases and as required by tax and accounting law.
  • Support email: up to two years.
  • Advertising data: held by Google and its partners under their own retention policies.

12. Security

All data our apps send travels over HTTPS. We collect the minimum needed, hold no passwords or payment data, and limit access to our systems to the people who maintain them. No system is perfectly secure; if a breach affects you we will tell you as the law requires.
